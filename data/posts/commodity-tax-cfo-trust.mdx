In September 2022 I joined RBC&rsquo;s CFO Group as a Senior Data Scientist. Nobody had asked me to build AI. I was hired to help automate the Commodity Tax return process, a manual workflow that consumed finance analysts for months per cycle.

By Q4 2023, the process took 90 minutes. The metric earned a CFO Group RBC Quarterly Team Award. More importantly, it earned the credibility to propose Aegis, Astraeus, and eventually PAR Assist, a cascade that shaped the next three years.

This is the story of how automating a tax process became a stakeholder-trust project in disguise, and the architectural decision that made it land. The strategy compresses into one move: when an analyst questions a number, make the recorded path available for joint inspection instead of asking them to accept a final output on faith.

## The problem

Commodity tax returns at a major Canadian bank are not small paperwork. Finance teams pull transactions from General Ledger journals (~10&ndash;50 million rows per cycle), reconcile them against tax categories, and produce returns that reach CFO-level sign-off. The total tax allocation runs to roughly $600M per cycle. The process had accumulated years of institutional knowledge: undocumented edge cases, manual overrides, tribal understanding of which GL accounts mapped to which tax buckets.

Automating it wasn&rsquo;t &ldquo;wrap a Python script around Excel.&rdquo; It meant codifying tribal knowledge without breaking anyone&rsquo;s trust in the numbers.

## First project, first audition

When you join an organization as the AI person and your first assignment is process automation, there are two ways to read it. You can treat it as a detour: &ldquo;I&rsquo;ll do this so I can get to the real AI work.&rdquo; Or you can treat it as an audition.

I treated it as an audition. Finance leaders were not going to green-light a machine-learning platform for CFO-level decisions on the strength of my CV. They were going to green-light it on the strength of my first delivery.

That framing changed how I built.

## The architecture decision that mattered

The obvious technical stack was straightforward: PySpark on CDP for the extraction (GL journals are too big for pandas at this scale), orchestration around the tax category mappings, output artifacts the finance team could hand off.

The decision that mattered wasn&rsquo;t technical. It was architectural-as-stakeholder-management.

I built Tableau dashboards **not as the calculator, but as configured inspection surfaces beside the pipeline.**

Relevant stages exposed recorded state through Tableau. When a number looked wrong, an authorized analyst could follow an available lineage path toward associated journal rows and see the recorded mapping outcome or version. The views exposed evidence for investigation; they did not validate the source population, mapping logic, joins, or resulting amount.

## Where the approach proved useful

The transparency layer gave analysts and the delivery team a shared review surface. A questioned aggregate could move through inspection, an available recorded path, reconciliation or an approved correction, and a reviewed rerun.

The dashboards did not establish correctness on their own: source quality, rule quality, access controls, and complete lineage still mattered. They made an anomaly easier to localize and discuss than a final spreadsheet or PDF would have. By Q4 2023, the cycle took 90 minutes and the work received a CFO Group RBC Quarterly Team Award; the interface was one contributor to the review workflow, not the sole cause of that result.

## The result

- **Months → 90 minutes** per cycle
- **CFO Group RBC Quarterly Team Award** (Q4 2023)
- A production pipeline with Tableau dashboards supporting configured monitoring and inspection
- A finance team that started bringing ideas to the AI team, not the other way around

## What I&rsquo;d do differently

One scope call I&rsquo;d revisit: the first dashboard set deliberately did not surface the category-mapping rule book. Analysts could follow supported paths from a final number toward associated journal rows, but the *rules* that produced each mapping lived in Python code. Updating a governed rule therefore required a code change and redeploy.

In retrospect I&rsquo;d have built a *mapping-rules dashboard* earlier, letting analysts propose rule additions directly while retaining code-review approval before production. That would have turned the mapping layer into a shared, governed surface and reduced coordination and redeployment work. The transparency-as-architecture principle worked for *outputs*; I should have applied it to the *rules* too.

## What this opened

By the time Commodity Tax was running cleanly, the permission structure inside the CFO Group had shifted. I could propose Aegis v1 (a rules-based benchmarking engine for the Big 6 Canadian banks) and get the air cover to build it. When Aegis v1 shipped, I could propose Aegis v2, the AI-native refactor, and deliver it in two weeks while Astraeus productionization was already underway. When Astraeus reached production scale, I had the credibility to conceive PAR Assist, hand the concept to the 2025 Amplify cohort for problem-space ideation, and then build the production platform end-to-end for a full CFO Group launch across all geographies.

Every one of those projects was technically ambitious. None of them would have been greenlit by a new hire with nothing to show for it. Commodity Tax was underwriting every subsequent bet.

## The pattern

Your first project at a new organization isn&rsquo;t just a deliverable. It&rsquo;s the audition that determines which doors open next.

The architectural decision (Tableau as a transparency layer, not an output) was the first time I applied a principle I&rsquo;d use in every subsequent system: **the hardest thing in production AI isn&rsquo;t getting the answer right; it&rsquo;s getting the humans who own the decision to trust the system that produced it.**

Astraeus&rsquo;s LLM-routes-intent / deterministic-code-handles-calculation separation is a descendant of this idea. Aegis v2&rsquo;s confidence-thresholded clarification is a descendant of this idea. It would rather ask than confidently mis-resolve. PAR Assist&rsquo;s registered tool path writes structured records for successful field-assignment calls. In regulated production systems, the architecture has to support inspection, testing, and stakeholder review.

Commodity Tax was where I learned that lesson.

The technology changes. The pattern doesn&rsquo;t.
