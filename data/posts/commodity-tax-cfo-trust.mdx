My first delivery in RBC&rsquo;s CFO Group reduced a months-long Commodity Tax process to roughly 90 minutes. The work supported an allocation of roughly $600M and received a CFO Group RBC Quarterly Team Award in Q4 2023.

The result mattered. The way the finance team could question it mattered just as much.

Commodity Tax was a data and process-automation problem, not an AI project. Its central design decision was to build calculation and inspection together: PySpark performed the governed transformation, while configured Tableau views gave analysts a practical place to investigate a questioned value, reconcile a mapping, and review a rerun.

## The first assignment

I joined the CFO Group in September 2022 as a Senior Data Scientist. The Commodity Tax workflow moved through General Ledger extraction, reconciliation, category mapping, aggregation, and return preparation. Much of the process depended on spreadsheets, manual handoffs, and mappings whose exceptions were understood by experienced finance partners.

Automating that workflow required more than translating formulas into code. A mapping could be mechanically consistent and still misrepresent the business rule. A clean aggregate could still inherit a missing source record. The system had to make the calculation repeatable while preserving a route for the people who owned the process to challenge it.

That made the collaboration model part of the architecture. I led development and stakeholder engagement, working with finance partners who supplied the meaning of categories and exceptions. The delivery team turned those decisions into reviewed mappings and transformations. Analysts remained responsible for investigating anomalies and approving corrections.

## Calculation and inspection were one design problem

The compute path used PySpark to move the process through five conceptual stages: Extract, Reconcile, Category Map, Aggregate, and Return. The inspection path sat beside the relevant stages rather than appearing only after the final artifact.

Keeping calculation and inspection separate prevented the dashboard from becoming a second calculator. Tableau displayed configured views of recorded pipeline state. It could help an authorized analyst inspect an associated mapping or source record where the retained links supported that path. It did not prove that the source population was complete, the mapping rule was correct, or the returned amount deserved approval.

The inspection surface helped the team ask a better question: where did this value come from, and what evidence is available to investigate it?

## What happened when a number was questioned

A questioned return value did not have to become a debate about the whole pipeline. The analyst could begin with the aggregate, follow the recorded associations that were available, inspect the relevant mapping and source records, and identify whether the issue was data, configuration, or interpretation.

If the evidence supported a correction, the governed mapping or source issue could be addressed and the process rerun for review. If the recorded path was incomplete or inconsistent, the right outcome was further investigation rather than a confident explanation.

The loop gave finance and engineering a shared object to inspect. Instead of handing over an opaque final number, the delivery team could narrow the disagreement to a stage, mapping, or missing association. That made review more efficient without turning visibility into a correctness guarantee.

## The outcome and its limits

By Q4 2023, the process completed in roughly 90 minutes rather than months. The work received the team award, and the pipeline remained part of the operating process with analysts reviewing and correcting issues where needed.

The outcome came from the full delivery: codified mappings, PySpark processing, configured inspection, finance partnership, testing, and operating review.

The recorded path also remained conditional. A join without retained identifiers, a changed rule without the matching version, or an omitted source record could break the investigation trail. The system made supported questions easier to trace; it did not create perfect provenance.

## What I would change

I would expose the governed mapping-rule surface earlier.

The initial design let analysts inspect supported paths from a returned value toward associated records, but mapping changes still moved through code and redeployment. A reviewed interface for proposing rule changes could have made that collaboration more direct while preserving approval and version control.

Business users should not edit production rules without controls. A reviewed interface would make the ownership boundary visible: finance proposes or explains the rule, engineering validates the implementation, and the released version remains traceable to both.

## What the delivery taught me

Commodity Tax was my first substantial delivery in the group. In financial automation, an answer becomes useful when the people accountable for it can inspect the available evidence, challenge the rule, and correct the process without treating the software as an oracle.
